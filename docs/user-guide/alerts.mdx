Alerts notify you by email when a flow in your project fails. Instead of manually checking the runs page, you can add email addresses that automatically receive failure notifications.

## Accessing Alerts

1. Click the **gear icon** on your project (Project Settings)
2. Switch to the **Alerts** tab

## How Alerts Work

When a flow run fails, OpSyn sends an email to all configured alert recipients. To avoid email overload, alerts follow a smart frequency system:

<Note>
  **Alert Frequency**: You receive an email for the **first failure** of each flow per day. Subsequent failures from the same flow are batched and included in a **daily summary email** instead of sending individual notifications.
</Note>

## Adding Alert Emails

1. Open **Project Settings → Alerts**
2. Click **+ Add Email**
3. Enter the email address
4. Click **Add**

The email appears in the alert recipients list immediately. That address will start receiving failure notifications for all flows in the project.

### Who Can Receive Alerts?

Any valid email address can be added — it doesn't have to be a project member. This is useful for:
- Alerting on-call engineers
- Sending to a shared team inbox
- Notifying stakeholders outside the platform

## Removing Alert Emails

1. Open **Project Settings → Alerts**
2. Find the email in the recipients list
3. Click the **trash icon** next to it

The email is removed immediately and will no longer receive notifications.

## Alert Content

Each alert email includes:
- **Flow name** — Which flow failed
- **Failure time** — When the failure occurred
- **Failed step** — Which step in the flow caused the error
- **Link to the run** — Direct link to inspect the failed run

## Permissions

| Permission | Who Can |
|------------|---------|
| **View Alerts** | Anyone who can view project settings |
| **Add/Remove Alerts** | Users with `WRITE_ALERT` and `WRITE_PROJECT` permissions (typically Admins) |

<Tip>
  Set up alerts early for critical flows. Even if your flows are running fine now, having alerts configured means you'll be notified immediately if something breaks.
</Tip>
